# What to decide

Four pages. Each is something to settle, price or knowingly accept before you commit money.

**[Open Questions](unasked.md)** -- Nine items, mostly commercial. Who the buyer is, given that the thesis bills the budget holder for a benefit that accrues to an assessor. Whether this is a product or a consulting engagement, because that choice picks the architecture. What the GPU estate costs. Which signing algorithm the customer mandates, against a primitive that can't be reversed. Plus an unproven correctness defect: restoring the high side from backup resets the anti-rollback high-water mark, and an older, validly-signed bundle can then replay. Start here if you're deciding whether to fund this.

**[What Cannot Be Known](unanswerable.md)** -- Where the information is controlled, or was never measured, so more effort buys nothing. Six of the seven things you need to design a transfer format sit behind a clearance.

**[Why These Fail](history.md)** -- Four failures since 1968, and the diagnoses line up: optional adoption, no workflow analysis, product variety, operator turnover. The 1978 attempt retreated to a compromise that looks like modern platform engineering, forty years early.

**[Ecosystem Risk](ecosystem.md)** -- Twelve projects under 300 stars, nine of them on the critical path, and two with no substitute I could find. Some of the dead ones still carry five-figure star counts. In five cases the paid tier is the feature a regulated deployment needs.
