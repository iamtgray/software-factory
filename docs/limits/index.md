# What To Decide

Four pages, and every one of them is a decision -- something you have to settle, price or knowingly accept before you commit money.

**[Open Questions](unasked.md)** -- Nine items, mostly commercial. Who the buyer is, given that the thesis bills the budget holder for a benefit that accrues to an assessor. Whether this is a product or a consulting engagement, because that choice picks the architecture. What the GPU estate costs. Which signing algorithm the customer mandates, against a primitive that cannot be reversed. Plus a correctness defect I'm fairly confident is real, though I haven't built the thing to prove it: restoring the high side from backup resets the anti-rollback high-water mark, and an older, validly-signed bundle can then replay. Start here if you're deciding whether to fund this.

**[What Cannot Be Known](unanswerable.md)** -- Where the information is controlled, or where I can't find anyone who has measured it, so more effort buys nothing. Six of the seven things you need to design a transfer format sit behind a clearance. My view is that a design built on guesses at those six does active harm, which makes the real question who you go and ask.

**[Why These Fail](history.md)** -- The idea has failed four times since 1968, as far as I can trace, and the diagnoses line up: optional adoption, no workflow analysis, product variety, operator turnover. Which of those are you designing against? The compromise the 1978 attempt retreated to looks to me like modern platform engineering, forty years early.

**[Ecosystem Risk](ecosystem.md)** -- Twelve projects under 300 stars, nine of them on the critical path, and two where I couldn't find a substitute at all. Some well-known tools turn out to be dead despite their star counts. And every licence trap I looked at put the feature a regulated deployment needs behind the paid tier. Which of these do you carry, and which do you replace?
